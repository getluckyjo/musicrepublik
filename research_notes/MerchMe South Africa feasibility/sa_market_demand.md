## 1. SA social media audience by platform (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, X), with age and gender splits and the 18–34 share

### Takeaway
SA has about 29.1m social media user identities (Oct 2025, 44.9% of 64.9m people). TikTok is now the largest and fastest-growing ad audience: 29.1m adults, +33% year on year, 61% male. Instagram is much smaller at 8.6m, but grew 19% and skews female (55%), which matters because MerchMe's "link in bio" model depends on both platforms. About 18.6m South Africans are aged 18–34 (28.6% of the population).

### Cited Findings
- **Population and connectivity (DataReportal Digital 2026 South Africa, published 8 Nov 2025, data to Oct/late 2025):**
  - Population: 64.9m.
  - Median age: 28.7.
  - Urbanisation: 69.9%.
  - Internet users: 51.7m (79.6% penetration).
  - Cellular connections: 127m (196% of population).
  - Source: [DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-south-africa)
- **Social media user identities:** 29.1m in Oct 2025 (44.9% of population). Identities aged 18+ equal 64.8% of the 18+ population. — [DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-south-africa)
  - Jan 2025 comparison: 26.7m identities (41.5%). — [DataReportal 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-south-africa)
  - Caveat: identities are accounts, not unique people, and the two reports use different reference dates.
- **Population age structure (end-2025):** 13–17: 8.4%; 18–24: 11.3%; 25–34: 17.3%; 35–44: 15.9%; 45–54: 10.5%; 55–64: 7.4%; 65+: 6.9%. — [DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-south-africa)
- **Platform ad reach, late 2025** (all from [DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-south-africa)). Gender is for the adult ad audience.

### Inferences
- **People aged 18–34:** (11.3% + 17.3%) × 64.9m ≈ **18.6m** (18–24 ≈ 7.3m; 25–34 ≈ 11.2m).
- **Adults 18+:** (100% − 30.8% under 18) × 64.9m ≈ 44.9m.
- **TikTok equals the identities figure.** TikTok's adult reach (29.1m, 64.8% of adults) exactly matches DataReportal's identities figure. The identities number therefore appears to be anchored on TikTok's ad reach, so treat 29.1m as an upper-bound "reachable adults" proxy rather than a deduplicated count.
- **Different audiences:** Instagram (8.6m, female-skewed, higher-income) is the natural "link in bio to shop" audience. TikTok (29.1m, male-skewed, mass-market) is the discovery engine. A merch platform must convert TikTok attention into a mobile web checkout, because there is no native TikTok shopping in SA (see Q7).
- **X is shrinking** (-11.5%) and is a weak channel for SA fan commerce.

### Gaps
- No 2026 GWI or Meta/TikTok-published age split by platform for SA was found. The share of Instagram and TikTok users aged 18–34 in SA could not be sourced. Global norms suggest more than 60%, but that is unverified for SA.
- No mid-2026 DataReportal SA update (e.g. a "Digital 2026 July Statshot" country figure) was retrieved. The Oct 2025 figures are the latest used.

## 2. Size of the SA creator economy and influencer marketing market, and creator counts by tier

### Takeaway
Industry estimates put SA influencer marketing at about R0.5bn a year, with Statista forecasting about 10% CAGR to about R820m by 2029. The best creator-count data is Humanz's Sept 2026 report:
- At least 330,000 South Africans are registered as creators.
- About one in six (≈55,000) actively pursued paid brand work in the past year.
- Only about one in five has ever been paid cash.
- 55% of paid creators earn under R5,000 a month from content.

This is a large, under-monetised "up-and-coming" pool, which suits a zero-upfront merch product.

### Cited Findings
- **Humanz, "Paid to Create: The South African Creator Economy 2026" (published 2 Sept 2026).** Survey of about 1,200 SA creators (243 verified paid creators), cross-checked against more than R100m in verified creator payments over 12 months. All findings below are from [Humanz 2026](https://www.humanz.com/south-african-creator-economy-report-2026/).
  - **Creator count:**
    - At least 330,000 South Africans are registered on Humanz, about one in every 180 people. This is a minimum.
    - About one in six of them actively pursued brand campaign work in the past year.
  - **Payment status:** Only about one in five SA creators has ever been paid in cash for brand work. For most, brand work means gifted product.
  - **Earnings distribution:**
    - 55% of paid creators earn under R5,000 a month from content across all sources.
    - About one in six earns more than R15,000 a month.
    - A handful earn more than R60,000 a month.
  - **Career ladder:**
    - Median annual earnings rise 17x, from about R10,000 (first paid deal) to R167,000 (creators paid on 8+ campaigns a year, Humanz campaigns alone).
    - Average fees per campaign rise less than 40% across that range.
    - 159 creators earned over R180,000 and 45 earned over R360,000 in the year from Humanz campaigns alone. A small number earned more than R1m.
  - **Growth:**
    - Paid creator opportunities have more than doubled every year for four years.
    - The number of creators seeking that work grew faster, by an average factor of 3.2 a year.
  - **Market size:**
    - Industry estimates typically place SA influencer marketing at around half a billion rand a year.
    - Humanz says published estimates "almost certainly undercount" the market, because they exclude agency margins, product, events and direct brand-to-creator deals.
- **Statista Market Insights (2025):** SA influencer advertising ad spend of €27.55m in 2025, CAGR 9.99% (2025–29), reaching €40.32m by 2029. — [Statista](https://fr.statista.com/outlook/amo/advertising/influencer-advertising/south-africa)
  - Business Report quotes the same forecast family as US$30.23m (R560.64m) for 2025. — [Business Report, 29 Mar 2025](https://businessreport.co.za/opinion/2025-03-29-a-deep-dive-into-the-local-26-million-customer-social-media-market)
  - Bizcommunity gives about R820m by 2029 (+10% a year). — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/a-deep-dive-into-the-local-26-million-customer-social-media-market-473489a)
  - The sources use different currencies and FX rates but agree on a roughly R0.5–0.6bn base.
- **Humanz benchmark report:** the sixth annual "Influencer marketing benchmarks for South Africa in 2026" (median CPM, CPE, cost benchmarks) was published 9 Mar 2026. The numbers are gated in the PDF and not on the web page. — [Humanz benchmarks 2026](https://www.humanz.com/influencer-marketing-benchmarks-measurement-for-south-africa-in-2026/)
- **Tier distribution (dated, 2020, Instagram only):**
  - 44.7% of SA Instagram influencers are micro (5K–20K followers).
  - Tier definitions: nano 1K–5K, micro 5K–20K, mid-tier 20K–100K.
  - Nano engagement rate: 4.79%.
  - Over 48% of SA Instagram creators artificially inflate followers or engagement; about 5.9% buy followers.
  - 43.79% of creators are male. Core influencers are women aged 18–34.
  - Top categories: Lifestyle (23.61%), Music, Photography.
  - Source: [HypeAuditor, May 2020](https://blog.hypeauditor.com/state-of-influencer-marketing-in-south-africa/)
- **Global comparators (labelled global):**
  - Goldman Sachs estimated a creator economy of about US$250bn (2023), approaching US$480bn by 2027. It counts about 50m global creators, growing at a 10–20% CAGR. — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027)
  - Linktree's broader definition counts about 207m people building or monetising audiences. — [New Market Pitch summary](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/creator-economy-market-size)
  - Global brand spend on creators: US$32.6bn in 2026 (Influencer Marketing Hub, cited by Gigapay).
  - Micro and nano creators took 45.5% of influencer spend in 2026 (eMarketer, cited by Gigapay). — [Gigapay Global Creator Economy Report 2026](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/the-global-creator-economy-report-2026)

### Inferences
- **Creator funnel for MerchMe** (from Humanz ratios applied to 330,000):
  - About 55,000 creators were actively seeking paid work in the past year (one in six).
  - About 66,000 have ever been paid cash (one in five, if the survey ratio applies to the registered base).
  - A few hundred earn professional incomes (R180k+ a year).
  - The "mid-tier / up-and-coming" target is best proxied as the **≈55,000 active creators**. The full 330,000 is the outer TAM of creators.
- **Merch is unlikely to cannibalise brand work.** Because 55% earn under R5k a month and income predictability is rated 2.1/5 (see Q7), an owned, recurring revenue line like merch addresses the top stated pain point.
- **Small market, so the opportunity is fan-funded.** SA influencer ad spend (≈R0.5–0.6bn) is tiny next to SA online retail (R130bn). MerchMe's upside comes from fan wallets, not brand budgets.
- **Follower counts overstate buyers.** Because roughly half of SA Instagram creators showed signs of inflated metrics (2020 data), conversion assumptions should be haircut.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) SA count of creators by follower tier (nano/micro/mid/macro) on Instagram or TikTok was found. The HypeAuditor split is from 2020. Humanz's full PDF (gated) may contain tier data. Modash lists "471 micro influencers" with majority-SA audiences in its database, which is not a market count.
- No IAB SA, Meta, TikTok, Ornico or Webfluential 2025–26 SA creator-market study with public numbers was found. Humanz says its market context draws on "IAB South Africa research", but no IAB SA figures were retrievable.

## 3. SA music industry: recorded revenue, streaming, Amapiano and SA hip-hop, number of artists and DJs, live events, and the importance of merch

### Takeaway
SA is about 78% of sub-Saharan Africa's US$120m recorded-music market: roughly US$94m in 2025, growing 12.9%. SA artists earned more than R504m from Spotify alone in 2025 (+28%), with about 74% coming from listeners abroad. PwC (Oct 2026) forecasts SA live music growth at 5.7% CAGR, the fastest in the world. There is no SA-specific data on merch's share of artist income.

### Cited Findings
- **IFPI Global Music Report 2026 (published Mar 2026):**
  - Sub-Saharan Africa recorded music revenue rose 15.2% to US$120m in 2025.
  - SA was 78.1% of regional revenue and grew 12.9% in 2025. In 2024 it grew 14.4% and was 75% of the region.
  - Global revenues rose 6.4% to US$31.7bn.
  - Sources: [Bizcommunity, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/ifpi-global-music-report-2026-south-africa-dominates-sub-saharan-music-markets-with-781-share-400529a); [Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/385461); [IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)
  - SSA growth decelerated from 22.6% in the prior year. — [Downtown Music Africa](https://downtownmusic.africa/what-changed-between-ifpis-2025-and-2026-global-music-reports-and-why-africas-story-still-matters/)
- **SA in USD:** 78.1% × US$120m ≈ US$93.7m in 2025. This is a derived estimate. — [Creator Economy IIQ (Substack)](https://creatoreconomyiiq.substack.com/p/south-africa-on-track-to-become-africas)
- **RiSA/Omdia (Mar 2025, likely 2024 RiSA data):**
  - Revenue from digital formats and services rose 21.4% to ZAR827.2m (from ZAR681.3m).
  - Physical rose 41.3% to ZAR3.6m.
  - Source: [Omdia](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/om127789/south-africa-music-industry-update-march-2025)
  - Conflict: the RiSA digital figure (≈R0.83bn) is lower than IFPI-implied SA totals (≈US$78m for 2024 ≈ R1.4bn). IFPI's total likely includes performance rights and sync. Treat the two as different scopes.
- **Spotify Loud & Clear 2026 (data for 2025, released May 2026):**
  - SA artists generated more than R504m in Spotify royalties in 2025 (+28% YoY), nearly double 2023.
  - About 74% came from listeners outside SA.
  - Independent artists earned more than half of all royalties.
  - About 3,550 SA artists were added to editorial playlists.
  - SA artists were discovered by first-time listeners more than 1.6bn times.
  - Sources: [africa.com, 15 May 2026](https://africa.com/entertainment/south-african-artists-generated-over-zar-504m-on-spotify-in-2025); [Downtown Music Africa, 13 Aug 2026](https://downtownmusic.africa/south-african-music-generates-r504-million-on-spotify-what-it-means-for-rights-and-royalties/); [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/lifestyle/entertainment/spotify-loud-and-clear-music-report-unpacked/)
  - Growing local genres: cloud rap, pop country, worship. — [africa.com](https://africa.com/entertainment/south-african-artists-generated-over-zar-504m-on-spotify-in-2025)
- **Amapiano (Spotify newsroom, June 2024, 2023 data):**
  - 55% of all global Amapiano streams came from SA. Amapiano was 78% of all streams in sub-Saharan Africa.
  - 40% of Amapiano streams came from 18–24-year-olds.
  - Source: [Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2024-06-13/piano-to-the-world-amapianos-evolution-from-underground-movement-to-global-sensation/)
  - 84% of Amapiano streams on Spotify are on mobile. Streams grew from 34m (2019) to 102m (2020). — [Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2024-06-13/how-a-global-pandemic-pushed-amapiano-to-the-world/)
  - Social posts claim IFPI named Amapiano the fastest-growing genre in SSA in 2025. Treat as unverified. — [Instagram post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DWGuqCmiGZL/?hl=en)
- **PwC Africa Entertainment & Media Outlook 2026–2030 (released 13 Oct 2026):**
  - SA E&M revenue: US$16.6bn (R301.8bn) in 2025, rising to US$19.4bn (R352.4bn) in 2030 (3.2% CAGR).
  - SA music, radio and podcasts: R8.0bn (2025) to R10.6bn (2030).
  - SA "leads the world in forecast live music growth" at 5.7% CAGR vs 2.1% globally.
  - Source: [PwC SA press release](https://www.pwc.co.za/en/press-room/africa-entertainment-media-outlook.html)
  - SA digital music streaming revenue is forecast to grow from R2.1bn (2025) to R3.8bn (2030). This comes via an SAfm post citing PwC (secondary). — [SAfm on X](https://x.com/SAfmRadio/status/2112043958083731846)
- **PwC 2025–2029 edition (prior year):** SA live music ticket sales were US$76m (R1.4bn) in 2024, with a projected 5.9% CAGR to 2029. — [PwC Africa E&M Outlook page](https://www.pwc.com/ke/en/publications/entertainment-and-media-outlook.html)
- **Musician counts:**
  - SAMRO had 28,308 members (associate and full) in FY2025, licence revenue of R680.4m, and R630.2m available for distribution. — [SAMRO](https://www.samro.org.za/)
  - An older Wikipedia figure gives "more than 15,000" members (undated). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Music_Rights_Organisation)
- **Artist merch in SA:**
  - Streetwear brand GALXBOY was official merch partner for Scorpion Kings Live (Kabza De Small and DJ Maphorisa), with a limited-edition concert-only tee drop. Date not stated in snippet. — [10and5 via Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/10and5/posts/galxboy-just-tapped-in-as-the-official-merch-partner-for-scorpion-kings-live-wit/1530608327902742/)
  - Universal Music SA runs an official artist apparel store (Nasty C, Prince Kaybee, Brenda Fassie, Tshego). — [UMG Africa](https://umg.africa/collections/apparel)
- **Global merch context (labelled global/US):**
  - MIDiA reports the "expanded rights" category (labels' share of merch, live etc.) grew 21.5% in 2025.
  - In a survey of 1,000 US merch, physical-music or ticket buyers:
    - Just under half say merch is becoming unaffordable, and 39% sometimes feel their fandom is exploited.
    - Just under half mainly buy merch from bigger artists.
    - 16–24s over-index on hoodies, posters, keychains and jewellery rather than tees.
    - Hip hop is the number one genre for 16–19s.
  - Source: [MIDiA Research, Mar 2026](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/the-new-rules-of-musics-growth-engine-insights-from-midias-merch-and-ticket-buyer-survey)

### Inferences
- **SA recorded music in rand:** about US$94m ≈ R1.5–1.7bn (2025, depending on FX). Spotify alone paid SA artists R504m.
- **Diaspora demand:** 74% of Spotify royalties come from abroad, so a meaningful share of fan demand for SA artists is outside SA. Cross-border or diaspora shipping could matter for musicians, even if the SA-only model is the starting point.
- **Musician supply proxies** for MerchMe's "musicians" segment:
  - About 28,000 SAMRO members (songwriters and composers).
  - About 3,550 artists added to Spotify editorial playlists in 2025 (an "active, gaining traction" proxy).
- **Youth, mobile, social:** the Amapiano audience is young (40% aged 18–24) and mobile (84%). This matches a mobile shop linked from TikTok and Instagram. Hip hop's strength among teens (global MIDiA) matches the SA hip-hop audience.
- **Live is the merch moment.** Live music ticket sales are about R1.4bn and growing faster than anywhere else in the world. Pre-order or on-demand merch tied to gigs and festivals is a natural trigger.

### Gaps
- No SA-specific data on merch's share of artist income (survey, RiSA, SAMRO or Music In Africa) was found.
- No SA count of active independent artists or DJs was found. Spotify did not publish a "number of SA artists earning more than R1m" in public coverage.
- PwC 2026–2030 SA live music rand values for 2025 and 2030 were not on the press page (only the 5.7% CAGR).
- No SA festival merch sales figures were found (e.g. Cotton Fest, Rocking the Daisies, DStv Delicious).
- A Water & Music claim that merch is "usually around 70 percent" of artist revenue is unsourced and probably refers to touring. Excluded as unreliable.

## 4. SA e-commerce: total size, growth, fashion share, social commerce, mobile share, basket size, payments, delivery

### Takeaway
SA online retail reached about R130bn in 2025 (+35%) and is forecast at R159bn in 2026 (+22.5%), the first full year at 10% of retail. Clothing is the most-bought online category. Smartphones are used by 58% of online shoppers. Payments have shifted towards account-to-account methods (Instant EFT, Capitec Pay, PayShap), which now roughly equal cards, and cash on delivery (COD) is marginal. However, only 34.2% of adults shop online, and card declines and shipping fees are the top cart killers.

### Cited Findings
- **Market size (World Wide Worx with Mastercard, Peach Payments and Ask Afrika):**
  - 2024: R96bn (+35%, 8% of retail). 2025: >R130bn, nearly 10% of retail. Forecast R150bn+ and 12% of retail by 2027. — [World Wide Worx, 11 Sept 2025](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2025/)
  - 2026 forecast: about R159bn (+22.5%, +R29bn), the first full year at 10% of retail. Broader retail grew about 4% in Jan–May 2026. Online retail was R30.2bn in 2020. — [Mastercard press release, 2 Sept 2026](https://www.mastercard.com/news/eemea/en/newsroom/press-releases/en/2026/august/sa-online-retail-to-reach-r159-billion-in-2026/); [News24](https://www.news24.com/business/money/a-whopping-r159bn-to-be-spent-in-2026-as-online-retail-crosses-10-threshold-20260902-0779)
  - Growth history: 29.1% (2023), 35.2% (2024), 35.4% (2025), 22.5% (2026F). — [TechCentral, 2 Sept 2026](https://techcentral.co.za/online-retail-south-africa-2026-world-wide-worx/285678/)
  - Methodology (2026): almost 24,000 TGI interviews, a 1,400-person online shopper survey, and 100+ retailers. — [News24](https://www.news24.com/business/money/a-whopping-r159bn-to-be-spent-in-2026-as-online-retail-crosses-10-threshold-20260902-0779)
- **Penetration:**
  - 79.1% of SA adults browse the web regularly, but only 34.2% buy goods online. — [The Media Online, Oct 2026](https://themediaonline.co.za/2026/10/trust-key-to-converting-online-browsers-into-buyers-study-finds/)
  - Penetration is falling: 38.2% (2022), 36.6% (2024), 34.2% (2025).
  - By age (2025): 15–24: 35.1%; 25–34: 39.5% (highest); 35–44: 35.7%; 45–64: 32.5%; 65+: 19.6%.
  - By gender: male 36.9%, female 31.7%.
  - More than 60% of adults in households earning R40,000+ a month shop online.
  - Source: [Online Retail in SA 2026 report PDF](https://www.mastercard.com/news/media/hwgncwio/online-retail-in-south-africa-2026.pdf)
  - Arthur Goldstuck: "The proportion of adults shopping online has fallen marginally, while the amount they spend has risen sharply." — [TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/online-retail-south-africa-2026-world-wide-worx/285678/)
- **Fashion and apparel:**
  - Clothing is the leading category: about 36% of online shoppers buying it, up from 30.2% in 2024. Womenswear 19.7%, menswear 18.9%, childrenswear 14.4%. These figures appear to be shares of online shoppers buying each category, not shares of online sales value. — [WWW 2026 PDF](https://www.mastercard.com/news/media/hwgncwio/online-retail-in-south-africa-2026.pdf)
  - Shein and Temu reached an estimated R7.3bn turnover in 2024. That is 3.6% of the clothing, textile, footwear and leather (CTFL) market and "nearly 40% of online sales in the category". — [World Wide Worx 2025](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2025/)
  - TFG's Bash platform: +40% sales, 12% of group turnover (2025 report). — [World Wide Worx 2025](https://www.worldwideworx.com/onlineretailsa2025/)
  - TFG Africa online: +49.2%, 8.2% of divisional sales (FY2026). — [Mastercard 2026 release](https://www.mastercard.com/news/eemea/en/newsroom/press-releases/en/2026/august/sa-online-retail-to-reach-r159-billion-in-2026/)
  - Mr Price online: +4.9% (FY2026), with over 55% of online orders collected in store.
  - Woolworths Fashion, Beauty & Home online was flat at R881m.
  - Source for the two lines above: [WWW 2026 PDF](https://www.mastercard.com/news/media/hwgncwio/online-retail-in-south-africa-2026.pdf)
- **Cross-border slowdown after tax changes:**
  - Cross-border e-commerce growth slowed to 7% in 2025 (from 30–50% a year before 2024).
  - Shein growth fell to 11%. Temu averaged a 42% monthly decline in 2025.
  - About 19m cross-border orders = 18.6% of SA e-commerce transactions (2025).
  - Source: [WWW 2026 PDF](https://www.mastercard.com/news/media/hwgncwio/online-retail-in-south-africa-2026.pdf)
  - Shein is still used by 21.5% of online shoppers, the second most-used platform. — [TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/online-retail-south-africa-2026-world-wide-worx/285678/)
- **Devices:** smartphones are used by 57.9% of online shoppers, laptops 26.0%, tablets 10.4%, desktops 9.8% (2025). Browser purchasing exceeds app purchasing in 42 of 48 categories. — [WWW 2026 PDF](https://www.mastercard.com/news/media/hwgncwio/online-retail-in-south-africa-2026.pdf)
- **Social and chat commerce (2026 consumer survey):**

### Inferences
- **Online clothing market:** Shein and Temu's R7.3bn was "nearly 40%" of online clothing sales in 2024. That implies SA online clothing of roughly **R18–19bn in 2024** (about 19% of R96bn). With 2025–26 growth, online apparel is plausibly R22–28bn in 2026. This is a rough, inferred TAM for "apparel bought online".
- **Implied average order value:** 19m cross-border orders = 18.6% of transactions, which implies about 102m SA e-commerce transactions in 2025. Against R130bn, that is an average order of about **R1,270**. This average includes groceries and electronics. Apparel and merch baskets are likely lower (the monthly spend data shows about 49% spend R400 or less a month online).
- **Adult online shoppers:** 34.2% of about 45m adults ≈ **15m**. Among 25–34s (39.5% of about 11.2m) ≈ 4.4m.
- **Checkout design implications for MerchMe:**
  - Account-to-account payments (Instant EFT 16.4% + Capitec Pay 9.1% + PayShap 8.4% + manual EFT 4.2% ≈ 38%) now roughly equal cards (debit + saved + credit + virtual ≈ 39%). MerchMe needs card plus Instant EFT/Capitec Pay/PayShap at launch.
  - Guest checkout (no account) is essential.
  - Delivery fees need to be clear and low, and the flow must be mobile-browser first.
  - A WhatsApp-link flow is a known purchase path for 35–40% of 18–34 online shoppers.
- **Opening for local on-demand apparel:** customs changes have blunted Shein and Temu's price advantage. Local fulfilment combined with a "support your artist" message has a better position than in 2023–24.

## 6. SA consumer constraints: disposable income, unemployment, cost of living among 18–34s, data costs, trust and fraud

### Takeaway
Constraints are severe:
- Youth (15–34) unemployment is 47.4% (Q2 2026), and expanded unemployment overall is 46.3%.
- Consumer confidence is negative (-13 in Q3 2026).
- Only 17.6% of adults "definitely agree" online shopping is safe.

Entry-level mobile data is now technically affordable (R152 a month for 2GB, 1.6% of GNI per capita). Online shopping skews to households earning R40k+, so MerchMe's realistic buyer is the employed, urban 18–34 fan, not the median young South African.

## 7. SA-specific creator monetisation pain points (payouts, platform programmes unavailable in SA, brand-deal dependency)

### Takeaway
SA creators cannot access TikTok's main payout programme (Creator Rewards runs in only 7 countries, none in Africa) or TikTok Shop. They depend on gifted product and one-off brand deals:
- Only about one in five creators has ever been paid cash.
- Half of those paid once are never rebooked that year.
- 55% wait more than a month for payment or have to chase it.
- Income predictability is rated 2.1 out of 5.

Facebook content monetisation and YouTube's Partner Programme are the main platform-native options.

### Cited Findings
- **TikTok Creator Rewards Program:** open only to creators in the US, UK, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France and Brazil. — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-rewards-program)
- **OkayAfrica (Jan 2025):**
  - TikTok's monetisation schemes operate in only three African countries. In SA, Morocco and Egypt, the available scheme is the "Effect Creator Rewards" programme (for AR effects), not the video Creator Fund or Rewards.
  - Creator quotes: "I rely on brands. That's the only way I've made money" (Charity Ekezie); "The money I made from the platforms themselves has been so small that I can't even count it as real income" (Foyin Ogunrombi, SA-Nigerian beauty creator).
  - Source: [OkayAfrica, 22 Jan 2025](https://www.okayafrica.com/is-tiktok-excluding-africans-from-its-creator-economy/246881)
- **TikTok Shop:** not available in SA, per TikTok's own discovery pages. This is a weak, user-generated source; there is no official TikTok statement. — [TikTok discover](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/tiktok-shop-in-south-africa?lang=en)
- **Facebook Content Monetization:** ads are available to creators in SA, per Meta's country list. — [Meta Business Help Centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/267128784014981)
  - A creator-community post claims Facebook monetisation is available in only two African countries, Egypt and SA (unverified). — [Facebook post](https://www.facebook.com/BAPUSHAonline/posts/did-you-know-that-facebook-pays-for-content-creators-in-south-africa-facebookmon/1171821201612167/)
- **YouTube Partner Program:** requires living in a YPP-eligible country. Fan-funding features open at a lower threshold (1,000 subscribers); ad revenue sharing requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10m Shorts views. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851); [YouTube Creators](https://www.youtube.com/creators/earn/youtube-partner-program/)
  - SA creators report the same 1,000 subscribers / 4,000 hours thresholds apply. — [Facebook group Q&A](https://www.facebook.com/groups/AskNanima/posts/7525866764134418/)
- **Humanz 2026 (SA creators):** all findings below are from [Humanz 2026](https://www.humanz.com/south-african-creator-economy-report-2026/).
  - Unpredictable income is the number one problem at every level of success; creators rate income predictability 2.1/5.
  - 55% wait more than a month to be paid or must chase payment, and "the bigger the creator, the longer the wait".
  - Half of creators paid once are not paid again in the year.
  - 69% say the one-off deal, not low pay, is what brands get most wrong.
  - Only about one in five has ever been paid cash; most brand work is gifted product.
  - The full report includes a "financial services opportunity" section (gated).

### Inferences
- **The core pain is unpredictable income.** Brand deals (one-off, slow-paying) and no TikTok payouts leave SA creators with few owned revenue streams. A zero-cost merch store linked from TikTok and Instagram offers an income line that does not depend on brands or platform payout programmes. This directly targets the top pain point (income predictability 2.1/5).
- **Payout speed and transparency are a differentiator.** With 55% of creators waiting more than a month for brand payments, fast and visible creator payouts (e.g. weekly EFT) would be a strong selling point.
- **No native shopping on TikTok.** Without TikTok Shop in SA, creators need an external, mobile-optimised checkout link. That is the gap MerchMe fills.

### Gaps
- YouTube's official YPP country-availability list was not retrieved directly. SA eligibility is inferred from creator reports and is very likely, but not verified from the Google list page.
- TikTok LIVE Gifts and Subscriptions availability in SA was not confirmed.
- Instagram monetisation tools (Subscriptions, Gifts, Bonuses) availability in SA was not confirmed.
- No SA data on creators' payout or banking friction (e.g. PayPal limits, FX on international platform payouts) was found beyond Humanz's payment-delay finding.
